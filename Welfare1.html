Forms of “Redistributive” Policy

An interactive reading of Joseph Heath’s Figure 4.1. s is the market status quo; every policy option a–e is measured relative to it. Hover or tap any point to see how the lines classify it.

Legend

Equality lineWhere rich and poor are equal. s and every option sit to its right (rich still ahead) — the closer to the line, the more equal.
Zero-net-welfare lineTotal welfare is unchanged along it. Above-right = net gain; below-left = net loss.
Constant-inequality lineRuns through s and a; the rich–poor gap stays exactly as at s. Crossing above it means less inequality.
Points s, a–eThe status quo and five policy options. Hover or tap any point.

Reading the axes

  • → rich better off
  • ← rich worse off
  • ↑ poor better off
  • ↓ poor worse off

Origin sits at the bottom-left; only this upper-right quadrant is shown. Faint guides through s mark no change for the rich (vertical) or the poor (horizontal).

In Heath’s argument, the egalitarian model pictures the welfare state pushing s toward c, d, even e. The public-economics view says it actually moves society toward a and b — and that where it is egalitarian, that shows up as a preference for b.
