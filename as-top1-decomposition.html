Why do top-1% income-share estimates differ?

From the same fiscal income to each definition of "expanded" income — Auten-Splinter vs CBO, adjustment by adjustment

Headline top-1% income-share numbers disagree because researchers adjust raw tax (fiscal) income in different ways to approximate a fuller measure of income. This chart breaks the disagreement down: both the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) and Auten-Splinter (AS) start from the identical fiscal-income top-1% share and apply a sequence of adjustments to reach their "expanded income" definition.

Each bar is one adjustment's effect on the top-1% share, in percentage points (negative = lowers the top-1% share). The story lives in the steps CBO leaves out: underreported income, removing realized capital gains, and adding corporate retained earnings. Applying those is most of why AS's top-1% share ends up below CBO's.

Sources — every series, with exact citation

  • Paper: Auten, Gerald & David Splinter (2019). "Top 1 Percent Income Shares: Comparing Estimates Using Tax Data," AEA Papers and Proceedings 109: 307–311. doi.org/10.1257/pandp.20191038.
  • Data file: companion workbook AutenSplinter-Top1percent-AEA.xlsx, sheets Decomp2014 and Decomp1979 — Panel A (per-step changes, CBO & AS columns) and Panel B (start/total/end anchors).
  • Concept: "Fiscal income" = income reported on tax returns; "expanded income" adds employer benefits, untaxed transfers, corporate retained earnings, etc. CBO and AS make different choices, especially on underreported income and capital-gains treatment.
