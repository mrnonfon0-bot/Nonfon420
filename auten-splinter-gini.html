Gini coefficients of US income — three measures, 1960–2022

Auten & Splinter (2025 update) — pre-tax, pre-tax + transfers, after-tax

The Gini coefficient is a summary statistic of an entire income distribution: 0 means perfectly equal, 1 means one person has everything. It's the most-cited single number for inequality after the top-1% share. Auten-Splinter publish three parallel Gini series — one per income definition.

All three series rise from roughly 1960 to today, but at very different levels. Pre-tax Gini (highest) rises from ~0.44 in 1960 to ~0.55 in 2022. Pre-tax + transfers sits 4–7 points lower (transfers compress the bottom of the distribution upward). After-tax Gini sits another 7–10 points lower again (taxes paid by high earners compress the top of the distribution downward).

The gap between pre-tax and after-tax Gini measures the redistributive effect of the US fiscal system. That gap has grown modestly over time as transfer programs (Medicaid, EITC, ACA) expanded — but the underlying market distribution has worsened faster, so total post-tax inequality still rises.
