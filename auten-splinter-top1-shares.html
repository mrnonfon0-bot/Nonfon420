Auten & Splinter — Top 1% US income share, 1960–2022

Three income measures: pre-tax, pre-tax + transfers, after-tax (from AS-Update-2025)

This chart shows the Auten-Splinter time series for the top 1% US income share under three different income definitions, every year (where available) from 1960 to 2022. Source: AS-Update-2025 Figure 1 master data.

The three measures answer different questions. Pre-tax income (highest line) ignores both transfers and taxes — closest to what the labor market and capital markets directly distribute. Pre-tax plus transfers adds Social Security, Medicare, SNAP, EITC etc., showing what households receive before paying federal income or payroll taxes. After-tax income (lowest line) shows the final distribution after all federal/state/local taxes and transfers — the closest measure to consumption capacity.

The gap between pre-tax and after-tax reflects the redistributive effect of the tax-and-transfer system. That gap is roughly stable in percentage points but the underlying levels rise: top-1% pre-tax share went from ~10% (1960) to ~15% (recent years), while after-tax share went from ~8% to ~9-10%.

Years with gaps in the source (early decades) are skipped. Hover any point for exact values.

Sources — every line, with exact citation

  • AS 2024 (JPE): Auten, G. & Splinter, D. (2024). "Income Inequality in the United States: Using Tax Data to Measure Long-Term Trends," Journal of Political Economy 132(7), 2179–2227. davidsplinter.com (PDF).
  • AS-Update-2025: Auten, G. & Splinter, D. (2025). "Income Inequality in the United States: Update," working paper July 2025. davidsplinter.com (PDF). Extends and refines the JPE 2024 estimates.
  • Data file: AS-Update-2025.xlsx, sheet F1 "Figure 1: Top 1% income shares and Gini coefficients." Rows 34+ contain the year-by-year series. Three columns mapped to the three series on this chart.
  • Methodology: AS use pre-tax/after-transfer national income as the denominator (the JPE 2024 methodology), with the 6 additional updates from the 2025 paper applied. Tax-unit basis with allocations. See AS 2024 §III for the income concept and AS-Update-2025 for the updates.
How this chart was produced. Time series read programmatically from AS's published xlsx — no pixel tracing.

  • Downloaded AS-Update-2025.xlsx from davidsplinter.com on 2026-05-28.
  • Read sheet F1 via openpyxl, starting at row 34 (year 1960) through 2022.
  • Three series captured: pre-tax (col B), pre-tax+transfers (col C), after-tax (col D). Companion Gini coefficient columns (E/F/G) included in the CSV but not on this chart for clarity.
  • Decimal fractions converted to percentages (×100).
  • Output: data/auten-splinter-2025-update/top1-shares-and-gini.csv.
