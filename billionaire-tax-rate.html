Top 400 effective tax rates — Splinter (2025) comment on BSYZ

Successive adjustments to the BSYZ "top 400 by wealth" tax-rate estimate, 2018–2020 averages

This chart recreates Table 1 of David Splinter's August 2025 comment on Balkir, Saez, Yagan & Zucman (2025) — "How Much Tax Do Billionaires Pay?" BSYZ estimated that the top 400 wealthiest US tax units paid an effective tax rate (24%) below the overall US rate (30%) in 2018–2020. Splinter argues the gap reverses sign after four adjustments. The bars show each adjustment in sequence.

The cumulative top-400 rate climbs from 24% → 27% → 35% → 35% → 38% as four corrections are applied: removing pure capital gains (double-counting fix), spreading Forbes wealth across multiple tax returns (dynastic family correction), adding transfers + refundable credits (overall rate adjustment), and reinstating missing SALT-cap state income taxes. The two ALT bars at the right are top-400 tax-and-giving rates — adding annual charitable contributions and end-of-life bequests respectively.

Scope note. Tax measure = federal + state + local taxes; income measure follows BSYZ's economic-income framework. The point of this chart is the methodological sensitivity at the very top of the distribution — small definitional choices move the top-400 rate by 13+ percentage points. Compare cautiously with the bottom-of-distribution sources that share none of these definitional issues.

Each bar is the top-400 tax rate after applying one additional adjustment to the BSYZ baseline. The dashed line shows the overall US tax rate (30% before adjustments, 25% after adding transfers/credits).
