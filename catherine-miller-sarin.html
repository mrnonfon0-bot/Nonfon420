Does Social Security erase the rise in wealth inequality?

Top wealth share by SCF wave, 1989–2019 — marketable wealth alone vs including Social Security — Catherine, Miller & Sarin (2025, Journal of Finance)

Catherine, Miller & Sarin take the famous finding that US wealth has become much more concentrated since the 1980s — and ask what happens once you count the wealth most families actually rely on: their future Social Security benefits. They value accrued benefits with market yield curves in the Survey of Consumer Finances.

By the standard marketable-wealth measure (red — essentially the Saez–Zucman concept), the top 10% share climbed 62.0% → 71.5% (+9.5 pp) from 1989 to 2019. Once Social Security wealth is included (blue), the same share barely moves — 55.7% → 57.4% (+1.7 pp) — and spends most of the 1990s below its 1989 level. The steep red climb flattens out.

Their conclusion: conventional measures, by excluding the safety net, overstate the rise in wealth concentration. This is the direct counterpoint to the Saez–Zucman wealth chart in this gallery — same starting data, opposite headline once entitlements are counted. Toggle between the top 10% and top 1%.

This is the paper's Figure 3, recomputed point-by-point from the authors' official replication microdata (SCF person-level file with each wave's Social Security wealth, plus DB pension wealth and the Forbes 400). "Marketable wealth" here includes DB pensions and Forbes-400 wealth but excludes Social Security. The attenuation is robust across 14 further specifications (funding-gap benefit cuts, tax hikes, liquidity premia, heterogeneous discount rates) — those Table III variants are in the underlying CSVs. Compare with the Rettenmaier–Jansen and Sabelhaus–Volz charts in this group.
Group:

One point per triennial Survey of Consumer Finances wave (11 waves, 1989–2019). The number at the right of each line is its 1989–2019 change in percentage points.

How this chart was produced. The plotted series are recomputed from the replication package's own microdata by scripts/build-catherine-miller-sarin.py, reproducing the package's Stata recipe (figure3_riskfree_and_risk_adjusted_top_shares.do → create_top_wealth_shares.do) in Python. No pixel tracing.
Show transformation steps
  • For each measure: total wealth = SCF net worth + Social Security wealth (ssw_acc_1 risk-free / ssw_acc_2 risk-adjusted / nothing for marketable-only).
  • Weighted percentile threshold per wave (Stata _pctile definition, person weights); top group = value above the 90th (or 99th) percentile boundary.
  • Group totals = Σ weight × wealth, plus DB pension wealth (both groups) and Forbes 400 wealth (top group), exactly as in the authors' .do file; share = top / (top + bottom).
  • All 12 Figure-3 endpoints cross-validated against the published Table III Panel A before charting (max deviation 0.06 pp).
  • Output: data/catherine-miller-sarin/figure3-top-shares.csv (this chart) + top-wealth-shares-ss.csv (all 14 Table III specifications) + metadata.json.
