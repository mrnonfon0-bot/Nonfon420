Forms of “Redistributive” Policy

An interactive reading of Joseph Heath’s Figure 4.1. s is the market status quo; every policy option a–e is measured relative to it. Hover or tap a point to see how the two diagonals classify it.

Legend

Equality lineRich and poor hold equal amounts. Moving toward its upper-left side is more egalitarian.
Zero-net-welfare lineTotal welfare is constant. Upper-right of it = net gain; lower-left = net loss.
Status quo (s)The market outcome. Every option a–e is measured relative to s.

Reading the axes

  • → rich better off
  • ← rich worse off
  • ↑ poor better off
  • ↓ poor worse off

In Heath’s argument, the egalitarian model pictures the welfare state pushing s toward c, d, even e. The public-economics view says it actually moves society toward a and b — and that where it is egalitarian, that shows up as a preference for b.
