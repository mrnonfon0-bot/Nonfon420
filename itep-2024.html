Federal + state & local taxes as a share of income, 2024

ITEP "Who Pays Taxes in America" — by household income quintile, with top 1% broken out

This chart recreates Figure 3 of ITEP's April 2024 report "Who Pays Taxes in America in 2024." ITEP models the full US tax burden — federal + state + local, including income, payroll, property, sales, excise, and corporate taxes — and reports it as a share of a broad income measure (including employer-paid FICA and corporate profits).

The stacked bars decompose total effective tax rate into the federal piece and the state & local piece. The structural pattern: total tax rates rise modestly from ~17% at the bottom to ~35% at the top. The federal share is progressive (3.6% → 25.5%); the state & local share is mildly regressive (13.5% → 9.2%) — sales, excise, and property taxes weigh harder on lower-income households.

Scope note. This is ITEP's all-government, all-tax measure — broader than the federal-only series from CBO / TPC / Splinter / Auten-Splinter elsewhere on this dashboard. Compare ITEP totals against PSZ 2018 (also all-government); compare ITEP's "federal" column against the federal-only sources.

Each stack shows federal vs state & local tax burden. Hover any segment for exact numbers.
