Non-elderly middle-class federal tax rate, 1979–2016

Looney, Larrimore & Splinter (2020) — Middle Class Redistribution, Figure 8

This chart recreates Figure 8 of Looney, Larrimore & Splinter's 2020 "Middle Class Redistribution": federal taxes as a share of market income for the non-elderly middle class (the middle three income quintiles of non-elderly households), every year from 1979 to 2016 — and broken down by the four federal tax sources.

The total middle-class federal tax rate fell from about 21.1% of market income in 1979 to 17.5% in 2016 (a 3.6 percentage-point drop). The composition shifted too: the individual income tax share fell most (from ~8.6% to ~3.6%, driven by expanded credits and rate cuts), while the payroll tax share rose (from ~9.5% to ~11.8%). The authors use this to argue that middle-class federal taxes have declined, not risen, over four decades.

Toggle between the total and the by-source breakdown. Hover any year for the exact figures.

Scope: federal taxes only (individual income + payroll + corporate + excise), as a share of market income, for the non-elderly middle class. The market-income denominator (no transfers added) differs from CBO's "income before transfers and taxes" denominator — so levels aren't directly comparable to the CBO quintile chart; read this as the middle-class trend and composition on LLS's basis. Underlying data: CBO (2019).
