How much inequality could a society sustain?

Observed Gini vs the maximum feasible Gini, pre-industrial societies and their modern counterparts — Milanovic, Lindert & Williamson (2011)

Many pre-industrial societies had income Ginis in the 40s–50s, similar to a developing country today. But a near-subsistence economy cannot sustain as much inequality: if the average person lives close to survival, there is only so much surplus an elite can take. The authors formalise this ceiling as the Inequality Possibility Frontier (IPF) — the maximum feasible Gini at a given average income.

Each dot is one society: the x-axis is average income as a multiple of subsistence, the y-axis is the observed Gini. The dashed line is the maximum feasible Gini (IPF). Historical societies (blue) cluster up near the ceiling — they extracted most of the inequality their economies could bear. Modern counterparts (orange), far richer, sit well below it. The vertical gap to the line is "unused" inequality; the extraction ratio (hover) is how close to the ceiling each society reached.

Hover any point for the society, observed Gini, feasible-maximum Gini, and extraction ratio. Income is on a log scale (multiple of subsistence minimum).

Table 2 covers ~28 pre-industrial social tables (Rome 14 AD onward) plus modern counterpart countries (c. 1998–2004). All plotted rows were validated against the definitional identity extraction ratio = Gini / IPF.

Sources — with exact citation

  • Paper: Milanovic, Branko, Peter H. Lindert & Jeffrey G. Williamson (2011). "Pre-Industrial Inequality," The Economic Journal 121(551): 255–272. doi:10.1111/j.1468-0297.2010.02403.x.
  • Data: Table 2 — observed Gini, average income (multiple of subsistence), maximum feasible (IPF) Gini, and inequality extraction ratio, for pre-industrial societies and modern counterparts.
  • Underlying data: historical social tables compiled by the authors (Global Price and Income History Group, gpih.ucdavis.edu).
How this chart was produced. Parsed from Table 2 text via scripts/build-milanovic.py. Every plotted row was kept only if it satisfied the definitional identity extraction ratio = Gini / IPF (tolerance 1.5), which both filters noise and confirms the columns were read correctly. No pixel tracing.
