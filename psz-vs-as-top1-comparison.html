PSZ vs Auten-Splinter — top 1% pre-tax income share

The headline disagreement in US income-inequality measurement, 1960–2022

This is the most-discussed disagreement in modern US distributional economics. Piketty, Saez & Zucman (PSZ) and Auten & Splinter (AS) both build top-income-share series using IRS tax data and BEA national accounts — but they make different methodological choices about how to allocate retained corporate earnings, government interest, underreported income, and dependents. The result: PSZ shows a sharp rise in top-1% pre-tax share from ~11% in 1980 to ~19% by 2019. AS shows a far more modest rise — from ~9% to ~14% over the same period.

Both groups use the same underlying tax data. The disagreement is about methodology, not about facts on the ground. This chart plots the two pre-tax series side by side using each group's own published numbers.

Read this carefully. Both series claim to measure "pre-tax top 1% share of US national income," but the income concept underneath differs in several specific ways:
  • Sharing unit: PSZ uses equal-split adults (spouses' income split equally); AS uses tax units with allocations.
  • Retained corporate earnings: PSZ allocates these proportionally to capital ownership. AS allocates differently.
  • Underreporting: PSZ assumes much underreporting flows to the top. AS uses IRS audit study evidence and finds less of it does.
  • Dependents and non-filers: PSZ retains some dependent returns. AS removes them as not representing independent economic units.
See the AS 2024 JPE paper §IV for the head-to-head methodological accounting.

Two series, same metric. The gap is what the debate is about.
