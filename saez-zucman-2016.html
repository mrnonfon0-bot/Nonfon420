US wealth shares, 1913–2012 — Saez & Zucman (capitalization method)

Top 0.1% / 1% / 10% and bottom 90% shares of total household wealth

Saez & Zucman's landmark estimate of US wealth concentration, 1913–2012, built with the capitalization method: they take the capital income (dividends, interest, rents, business profits) reported on income-tax returns and capitalize it into the underlying wealth stocks. It is the most-cited — and most-debated — long-run US wealth series.

The headline is a U-shape: the top 0.1% wealth share fell from a ~25% peak around 1929 to about 7% by 1978, then climbed back to 22% by 2012 — nearly its pre-Depression high. The mirror image is the bottom 90%, whose share rose to ~36% in the mid-1980s before falling to 23% by 2012.

Click any series in the legend to isolate it. This is the SZ capitalization series; compare it with the WID and RealTime Inequality wealth charts in this gallery, which use related but distinct methods (levels differ).

Net household wealth, tax units. SZ 2016 capitalization estimates — later work (Smith–Zidar–Zwick 2021; Auten/Splinter) revises the very top shares somewhat downward by changing how fixed-income and business wealth are capitalized; this chart is SZ’s original series.

Sources — every series, with exact citation

  • Paper: Saez, Emmanuel & Gabriel Zucman (2016). "Wealth Inequality in the United States since 1913: Evidence from Capitalized Income Tax Data," Quarterly Journal of Economics 131(2): 519–578.
  • Data file: SaezZucman2015MainData.xlsx, sheet DataFig1-6-7b ("Top wealth shares, % of total household wealth"). Each line = one column of that table.
  • Project page: gabriel-zucman.eu/uswealth.
  • Method: Capitalization — capital income flows from income-tax data are capitalized into wealth stocks; tax units.
How this chart was produced. Read directly from SZ's published main-data workbook (sheet DataFig1-6-7b) via scripts/build-saez-zucman-2016.py; the extraction was validated against SZ's published anchors (top 0.1% ≈22.0% in 2012, ≈7.1% in 1978; top 1% ≈41.8% in 2012; top 10% ≈77.2% in 2012) before charting. The intro figures are computed from the data. No pixel tracing.
