Fraction of U.S. tax units paying no federal individual income tax, 1913–2015

A long-run time series from David Splinter (2020)

This chart recreates Figure 1 of David Splinter's 2020 Tax Notes Federal article "Who Pays No Federal Individual Income Tax?" The line shows what share of U.S. tax units owed zero federal individual income tax in each year — the bottom edge of the effective-tax-rate distribution, where ETR is by definition zero.

Two adjustments matter for interpretation. The raw IRS Statistics of Income data treats every filing unit identically, including filers younger than 20. Splinter publishes a parallel "20+" series that excludes those young filers from the denominator, which the early-period data otherwise overstates. From 1960 onward both series rest on annual microdata; before then they use Splinter's reconstruction notes (see the methodology details under the chart).

The big spike in the early 1940s reflects the WWII conversion of the federal income tax from a small "class tax" affecting roughly 5% of households into a "mass tax" reaching the majority — by 1944 only about 31% of filers paid no federal income tax. The post-1980 rise reflects the expansion of the EITC, refundable credits, and the 2008–2010 recession spike.
