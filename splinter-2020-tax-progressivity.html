Average federal tax rates by income quintile, 1979–2016

Splinter (2020 NTJ) Figure 2 — CBO data series

This chart recreates Figure 2 of David Splinter's 2020 National Tax Journal article "U.S. Tax Progressivity and Redistribution." It is a long-run time series of average federal tax rates for the bottom, middle, and top income quintiles. Splinter compiled the series; the underlying numbers come from the CBO's "Distribution of Household Income" annual reports.

What's a "federal tax rate" here? Total federal taxes (individual income + payroll + corporate + excise) divided by income, where income = market income plus social insurance benefits. The bottom quintile's rate is often negative after refundable credits (EITC, child tax credit) — those credits exceed taxes paid for many low-income households.

The structural story: the top quintile's rate dropped sharply from ~27% to ~24% in the early 1980s (Reagan tax cuts), partly recovered in the 1990s, then drifted down. The bottom quintile's rate fell from ~9% in 1979 to roughly 1–2% by 2016 as the EITC and child credit expanded. The gap between top and bottom (a rough progressivity measure) widened.
