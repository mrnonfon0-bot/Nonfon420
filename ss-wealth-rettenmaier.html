Does counting Social Security change the wealth-inequality story?

Top-10% share of US wealth, conventional net worth vs. a measure that includes accrued Social Security — Rettenmaier & Jansen (2022)

Standard wealth statistics count only marketable net worth and exclude the value of future Social Security benefits — on the logic that workers have no legal claim to them. But accrued benefits are large (~$41 trillion in 2019) and, because they matter proportionally far more to lower- and middle-wealth families, leaving them out makes wealth look more concentrated and rising faster.

Rettenmaier & Jansen recompute the top-10% wealth share treating accrued Social Security as wealth. The top 10% hold a roughly stable ~17.8% of Social Security benefits but a much larger share of marketable wealth. The result: on conventional net worth the top-10% share rose 63.1% → 70.8% (1989–2019), but counting Social Security it rose only 54.0% → 57.3% — cutting the measured increase by more than half (+7.7pp → +3.3pp).

Share of total US wealth held by the top 10% of families (SCF), benchmark years 1989 and 2019. The amber bars (including Social Security) are both lower and rise less steeply than the red bars (conventional).

Two-point comparison (1989 vs 2019) using the figures stated in the paper's abstract. The paper's Table 3 also reports the top-5% series and all eleven SCF waves 1989–2019; those are not charted here.

Sources — with exact citation

  • Paper: Rettenmaier, Andrew J. & Dennis W. Jansen (2022). "Elderly Entitlements and Wealth Inequality," Private Enterprise Research Center (PERC) Working Paper 2205, Texas A&M University.
  • Conventional: Abstract (p.1): "their share of conventionally measured net worth grow from 63.1% to 70.8%" (1989–2019). Restated p.4.
  • Including SS: Abstract (p.1): "the share of total wealth held by the top 10% was 54.0% in 1989 and 57.3% in 2019." Restated p.4 ("54% in 1989 to 57.3% in 2019").
  • Underlying data: Survey of Consumer Finances; Social Security wealth = present value of accrued benefits (market-yield discounting).
